Before he was painting buildings eleven stories tall in Berlin, James Bullough was teaching middle school in Baltimore. In twenty ten he moved to Germany and taught himself to paint instead — realistic oil, learned by studying the Old Masters of the Netherlands.

Look up. That woman in the crimson dress is caught mid-leap, head tilted back, arms flung open. And she's coming apart. Bullough takes a photorealistic figure and shatters it into ribbon-like shards, so she seems to dissolve and reassemble in the same instant against the black.

His work hangs at Wynwood Walls in Miami, at Lollapalooza, on the URBAN NATION museum in Berlin. He came here for Shine in twenty twenty-two.

Follow one of those coral and navy strips as it peels off her body and drifts into the void. Watch how the color keeps moving after the figure stops.
