That beige Datsun two eighty Z is the point of the whole scene. This is a displaced Lao family finding refuge at Gandy Beach around nineteen eighty, and the car is where they gather — a fusion of car culture and pride. Look at the young men around it. One sits shirtless on the hood. Another leans against the door in a white t-shirt. Their clothes say togetherness, but notice how the gazes drift apart, each figure carrying a history the painting only hints at.

Michael Vasquez turns personal photographs into scenes like this, using clothing and landmarks to establish who someone is and where they come from. His work hangs in the Smithsonian's National Portrait Gallery and the Perez Art Museum. Swizz Beatz and Michael B. Jordan collect him.

Look past the car, into the background. Those are mangroves — the detail that roots this whole moment on the Florida coast...
