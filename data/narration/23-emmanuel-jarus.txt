She's fourteen stories tall, and Emmanuel Jairus won't tell you who she is. That's on purpose. This woman in flowing black is a figure he keeps returning to — he first painted her on a seaside building in Boulogne-sur-Mer, France. Now she's here, roughly a hundred eighty feet tall by sixty wide, the largest mural in SHINE's ten-year history. 

Jarus started out painting portraits on freight trains in Saskatchewan. They went viral online before he ever had a gallery show. He works with the plainest tools — bucket paint, brushes, rollers, sometimes brooms — and calls each painting an avatar for a city or a mood. 

He finished this one in twenty days, interrupted partway through when Hurricane Milton forced an evacuation. Look at her hand, resting on her chest. Her expression gives away nothing.  Perhaps a modern nod to Mona Lisa?
