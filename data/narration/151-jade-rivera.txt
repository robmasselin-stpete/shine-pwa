That blue ibis standing on the boy's chest is a messenger — in Jade Rivera's work, birds move between worlds, and they show up again and again, larger than life, watching over sleeping figures.

Rivera is a self-taught Peruvian muralist. He picked up graffiti at fourteen, has been painting walls since the late nineties, and now runs his own museum, gallery, and clothing line all on a single street in Lima. He's worked in more than seventeen countries. He painted this one for Shine, back in twenty sixteen.

It stretches forty-five feet — a boy on his back, eyes closed, held in dreamlike stillness against a deep blue-black. A monarch butterfly rests at his neck. The ibis bends its long neck toward a small pool of water.
