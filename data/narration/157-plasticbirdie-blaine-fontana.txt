This is the first public art project in the country funded by NOAA — the federal ocean agency. Stretch that fact across twenty-five feet tall and a hundred and seventy-five feet long, and you get the wall in front of you. The largest SHINE mural of twenty nineteen. Four hundred cans of spray paint, eight days.

Jeremy Nichols and Blaine Fontana, both based in Portland, painted it in partnership with Sea Walls, a program putting ocean science on public walls. The subject is Florida's small fishing towns and the fragility underneath them, borrowed from the look of old school-book infographics.

Start at the right, with the bald eagle — that sharp blue eye, the curved beak in warm golds and teal. Then follow it left, where the whole bird fractures into geometric triangles and dissolves into pure abstraction.
