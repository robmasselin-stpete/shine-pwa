That fawn is asleep, and there are white flowers growing right out of its back. They climb up the wall toward the window built into the design, blooming against a deep emerald green that turns the whole thing into a forest clearing.

Emily Ding paints wild creatures to tell stories about emotions we recognize but can't quite name. Look at her gallery titles and you get the theme fast: "Refuge." "Revival." "Resilience." "Rewilding."

She's based in Los Angeles, and her walls have gone up in Shanghai, Oakland, Austin, and Flint. She also works with Street Art for Mankind, a nonprofit that uses public art to fight child trafficking. This one she painted for SHINE, in twenty twenty-one.

Follow the flowers up from the sleeping fawn to where they meet the window. Then find her signature, down in the lower right.
