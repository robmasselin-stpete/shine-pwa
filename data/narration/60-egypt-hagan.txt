For Juneteenth in twenty twenty-one, Egypt Hagan painted a mural in Vermont commissioned by the state's Office of Racial Equity. The year after that, she brought her brushes here.

Look up. Two faces rise across the full height of the George F. Young building, wrapped in roses and soft washes of warm color. A hand is offering a rose, and another threatens with lightning.  Hagan works in portraiture as storytelling — bold color carrying narratives that go untold elsewhere. These aren't photographs on a wall. Look closely at the brushwork. It's loose and painterly, detailed enough to read as two specific people, but handled like fine art rather than a copy of a snapshot.

She's originally from Los Angeles, now based across the bay in Tampa, and her first solo show opened in New York back in twenty fourteen.
