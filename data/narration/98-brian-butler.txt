This one's a warning. Brian Butler called it "Carbon Punishment," and he painted it for Sea Walls — the ocean-conservation strand of SHINE twenty twenty. The subject is ocean acidification: what happens to the sea as it soaks up the carbon we put in the air.

He made the science visible. The sea life is painted in the colors of the pH scale, the same scale that measures how acidic the water is turning. The shells wear those acidic hues — and they're failing, riddled with polluted cracks and small skulls. A healthy ocean doesn't look like this.

That lettering is a choice too: jagged and heavy, a nod to Tampa Bay's death-metal roots. Butler even had two metal musicians write grindcore tracks to blast alongside the wall.
Look close at the shells. The cracks aren't decoration — that's carbon, doing its work.
