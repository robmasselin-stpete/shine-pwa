Two birds, facing each other, mid-greeting. On the left, a red one, its tail fanning out in wedges of orange, pink, magenta, and yellow. On the right, a blue one, all navy, teal, and green. Mirror poses. A conversation frozen on a cinder-block wall.

Cecilia Lueza painted this in twenty eighteen. Over the years it faded, and nearby construction chipped it up. So in twenty twenty-three she came back and restored it, funded through a National Endowment for the Arts grant. She and the ArtsXchange team could have designed something new. They kept this one. They were fond of it.

Lueza has nearly sixty public works to her name, from the Atlanta BeltLine to the North Carolina Museum of Art.

Look at how each feather radiates from a central spiral — one clean flat wedge of color at a time. Warm bird, cool bird, same open sky between them.  Check her other murals on 10th Street North and Downtown on the One, a residential tower - all stunning colors that jump out from the murals.
