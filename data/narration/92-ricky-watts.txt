Google made this artist their Artist in Residence. He painted the lobby of one of their Mountain View buildings — and here, for SHINE twenty twenty-one, he wrapped two full stories of The Factory in the same style.

Ricky Watts calls this technique hard-edge gradients. He developed it back in twenty eleven, working outward from letter-based canvas art into pure abstraction. What you're looking at is the result: giant fluid shapes, somewhere between liquid ribbons and inflated tubes, frozen mid-motion in orange, teal, coral, and deep blue.

His client list runs from Louis Vuitton to Mercedes to YouTube. Fifty-plus large murals since two thousand four.

Step back and take in how the forms overlap and flow around the corner of the building. Then notice the palm trees out front — the way their trunks cut across the composition. Watts painted the wall. The trees finish the effect.
