That calligraphy to the left reads "shang shan ruo shui" — the highest good is like water. It's a line from the Tao Te Ching, and it's the whole idea behind what you're looking at. A woman floats face-up in turquoise water, seen from above, her red dress spreading around her like a flower opening.

Lili Yuan paints this way in oil, on canvas, and each piece takes her more than a hundred hours. She left Hangzhou at thirteen, studied economics, and is largely self-taught as a painter. Water is a subject she returns to again and again.

She titled this one "Gift," painted for SHINE in twenty twenty.

Look at the light on the water — the ripples, the way it bends and pools across her dress. Then find the small red seal stamp down in the lower left corner. That's her signature.
