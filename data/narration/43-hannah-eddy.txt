That purple bird flying to the right, the one with a branch in its beak — that's a peace dove, reimagined as Florida wildlife. It fits. This wall belongs to the CASA Support Center, which helps survivors of domestic violence, and Hannah Eddy built the whole scene to feel welcoming to visitors.

She's based in Reno, trained in oil painting, then went straight to work for outdoor brands — Patagonia, Dakine, Sierra Nevada Brewing. Her style is pop-surrealism: bold black outlines, animals blown up to exaggerated scale, color that feels loud on purpose. She paints murals, she says, because no one has to pay to see them.

Look at how the color moves. The left side burns with orange, yellow, and pink. Then follow it right, where it thins to bare line work on the mint green — as if the color is still spreading across the wall.
