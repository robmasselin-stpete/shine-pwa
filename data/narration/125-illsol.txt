In the nineteen-nineties, the Florida panther nearly vanished. Fewer than two hundred and thirty adults were left in the wild. This one looms twenty-two feet over the intersection, and it is looking right at you.

That's the point. The title is "Notice Us" — a plea on behalf of Florida's wildlife, asking you to notice what development costs a fragile ecosystem.

The panther is the work of Illsol, a Tampa husband-and-wife team. The name means "illuminated soul." Michelle Sawyer trained as a tattoo artist. Tony Krol taught himself stencil work. Together they blend clean, photorealistic portraits with layered abstraction.

You can see both here. The face is rendered in exact detail — the fur, the whiskers, the gold-green eyes. Then everything dissolves into turquoise rings, coral halos, and monstera leaves.

Find the eyes. Hold their gaze for a second. That's what the artists want...
