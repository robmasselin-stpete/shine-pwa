Those rabbits didn't start here. This is the fourth chapter in an ongoing story about a young girl and the natural world — the first three were painted in Italy and China. The title comes from the White Stripes song.

Daas built these bunnies the way he builds everything: from interlocking angular planes, a style he developed during roughly a decade in Osaka, based on the crease patterns of Japanese origami. Soft grays, whites, lavenders, pale blues — fractured, cubist, and still unmistakably rabbits.

All of it is spray paint. Twenty-five feet tall, a hundred and three feet wide, finished in seven days.

Look at the background behind the animals — that patchwork of big triangles in golds, olive greens, corals, and tans. It reads like a quilt. Then find the blocky Dass tag up in the upper right corner, signing off on the whole warm geometric field...
