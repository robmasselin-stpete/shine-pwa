That perfume bottle floating four stories above you is underwater. So is the pineapple, the crystalline formations, the whole rippling teal scene — Finton Ma'Ghee painted it all submerged, with the light refracting down through a surface that isn't there.

Ma'Ghee grew up in New South Wales around the political murals of the Irish tradition, the son of a sculptor and an architect. He's painted in over twenty countries — a refugee camp girl in Jordan, endangered species in Bali. Journalists keep calling him the Australian Banksy. He calls that lazy.

This one went up for SHINE's tenth anniversary, in twenty twenty-four, after Hurricanes Helene and Milton pushed the festival back.

Look at the top edge, where the blue turns pale and bright. That's the water's surface, seen from below.
