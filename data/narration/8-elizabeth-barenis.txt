In two thousand eighteen, Elizabeth Barenis was one of just ten painters invited to the Mark Rothko Art Center in Latvia. What's in front of you now is her language for the tropics.

She calls it Abstract Precisionism — generalized shapes, clean lines that borrow from Cubism. Here that means oversized fan palm fronds, layered in green, teal, and soft yellow. Look at how the leaves overlap. The shapes read as translucent, one plane over another, and that stacking is what gives the wall its depth.

Barenis grew up in the Mississippi Delta, where her parents ran a music store. She moved to St. Petersburg in twenty fifteen and stayed for the arts community. She painted this for SHINE Origins twenty twenty-five, alongside a crew of other muralists.

Elizabeth is a great artist, we actually have one of her paintings in our own house.
