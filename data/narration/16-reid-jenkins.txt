Those flowers look painted from life, but the whole thing was outlined using a projection method — Reid Jenkins traced the arrangement onto the wall at building scale, then filled it in by hand.

The deep black background is the trick. It's the same move Dutch still-life painters made four hundred years ago, letting the darkness swallow everything so the blooms seem to glow. King proteas, red ginger, heliconia, orchids, ferns, all in crimson and coral and gold.

Jenkins is a St. Pete native. He came up through the arts program at Gibbs High, earned his degree in Baltimore, and now tattoos at a studio out in Leelman when he isn't painting walls this size.

Get close and look at the pincushion proteas — those spiky pink orbs. Every filament is its own brushstroke.  He calls It "Ikigai in Bloom"
