Every one of these orange faces is having a better day than you are. That's the point. Marina Capdevila paints old age as something loud and vital — her own grandmother is her muse, and she renders elderly people the way most art won't: mischievous, glamorous, fully alive.

She trained in Barcelona and Amsterdam, and she folds a Renaissance trick into all this chaos — sfumato, that soft blur the old masters used for shading, here wrapped around cartoon exaggeration and a lot of irony. She's painted in more than fifteen countries. This one she made for Shine in twenty twenty-two.

Look at the center. The giant face in the reflective sunglasses is calmly holding a lobster. To the right, someone in a bucket hat dangles a fish on a line. And up top, tiny palm trees, in case you forgot where you are.
