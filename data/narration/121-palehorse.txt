Every scale, every flame tip, every architectural ornament in front of you was drawn individually. Look closely — that intricate temple, those two coiling serpent-dragons, all of it rendered in fine linework against jet black.

This is "Temple of Eternal Bliss," painted by Palehorse in twenty nineteen with his brother Matt Parks. The name comes from a Sanskrit idea — Sat Chit Ananda: existence, consciousness, bliss. Palehorse isn't just borrowing the imagery. His practice is rooted in Kriya Yoga, and he hosts weekly meditation circles called Seekers of the Eternal.

By day, he's Chris Parks, who co-founded the SHINE mural festival and has drawn gig posters for the Foo Fighters, Iggy Pop, and Jimi Hendrix.

Find the thin white circle framing the central pagoda. It sits behind the spires like a halo, holding the whole composition together.
